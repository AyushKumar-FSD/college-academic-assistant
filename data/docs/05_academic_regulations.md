# B.Tech Academic Regulations (Nitte DU, CBCS, effective AY 2023-24)
Source: https://nitte.edu.in/nmamit/syllabus/ndu/2023-27/B.Tech._CS_Regulations_2023.pdf

These regulations are common to all B.Tech programs at NMAMIT, Nitte. They took effect on 01-08-2023 and were approved by the 54th Academic Council of Nitte DU on 24.06.2023. Older batches follow the earlier NMAMIT regulations (160-credit VTU scheme) published on the NMAMIT Rules & Regulations page.

## Attendance
- Each semester is a unit, and a student must have at least 85% attendance in each subject.
- The Principal can condone up to 10% of attendance for reasons such as medical grounds, participation in university-level sports or cultural activities, seminars, workshops and paper presentations.
- The college informs students of their attendance position in the first week of every month.
- A student with attendance below 75% in a course is not allowed to appear for the Semester End Examination (SEE) in that course and receives an 'N' grade. The course must be re-registered when it is next offered, in an odd, even or summer semester.
- Attendance at all CIE and SEE examinations is compulsory and there is no provision for re-examination. A student with a pending disciplinary action cannot attend any SEE in that semester.

## Credits and registration
- The B.Tech degree needs 160 credits (120 for lateral entry students). A student may register for 16 to 28 credits per semester (average about 20). In the summer semester the maximum is 16 credits.
- Registration is done at the start of every semester with the help of a Faculty Advisor, within 2-3 days of commencement. Late registration is allowed within one week of the last date on payment of a late fee.
- To register for a higher semester (3rd and above) a student must declare the intention at least two weeks before the end of the current semester, clear all college, hostel and library dues, pay the fees, and not be debarred.
- Courses with an 'F' grade are backlogs and must be re-registered. When a course is re-registered, its earlier evaluation marks are cancelled.
- Drop option: after a mid-semester review of CIE a student may drop poorly performing courses (down to the minimum credits); these do not appear on the grade card. Withdrawal near the end of the semester gives grade 'W'. A course can be taken for audit (grade 'U'), but core courses cannot be audited.
- Each semester has 20 weeks: 16 weeks of registration and course work and 4 weeks of exam preparation and examinations. The odd semester normally runs August to December and the even semester January to May. The optional summer semester is 8 weeks and is mainly for slow learners and failed students. Results are declared within 2 weeks of the last exam.

## Evaluation (CIE and SEE)
- Every course is evaluated for 100 marks: Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) 50% and Semester End Examination (SEE) 50%.
- For non-PBL courses CIE has 10 marks for quizzes, tutorials, assignments and seminars and 40 marks for two mid-semester examinations. For PBL/IPCC courses the theory part is 60% and the practical/PBL part 40%.
- Qualifying standard: at least 40% in CIE (20 of 50), at least 40% in SEE (20 of 50), and at least 40% overall (40 of 100) for a final pass.
- A student with CIE below 40% or attendance below 75% is not allowed to take the SEE and gets an 'N' grade.

## Grading
- Absolute grading: 90 and above = O (10 points); 80-89 = A+ (9); 70-79 = A (8); 60-69 = B+ (7); 55-59 = B (6); 50-54 = C (5); 40-49 = P (4); below 40 = F (0). AB means absent.
- SGPA is the credit-weighted average of grade points in a semester. CGPA is the same across all semesters, excluding F-grade courses.
- Class: CGPA 7.00 and above is First Class with Distinction; 6.00 to 6.99 is First Class; 5.00 to 5.99 is Second Class. A CGPA below 5.00 means Academic Probation, and the student can register for at most 16 credits in later semesters until CGPA crosses 5.0.
- Grade 'I': attendance 85% or more and CIE 70% or more, but absent from the SEE for a valid reason such as illness, accident or a family calamity. Documents must be submitted to the Controller of Examinations within 2 working days of the missed exam to write a make-up examination.
- Grade 'X': attendance 85% or more and CIE 70% or more, but SEE performance could result in an F. No F is awarded; the student gets a make-up chance. Outstanding 'I' and 'X' grades after the last scheduled make-up examination automatically become 'F'.
- Summer / fast-track: a student who satisfied CIE and attendance but got an F in the SEE can appear directly in the ensuing examinations as a backlog paper without re-registering. A student marked 'N' has to re-register for that course.
- Grace marks: up to 1% of the maximum total marks or 10 marks, whichever is less, with a maximum of 5 marks per course, only if the grace marks make the student pass that course.
- Re-evaluation is allowed only for theory papers, on application within the stipulated time with the prescribed fee. The marks after re-evaluation are final. A grade review appeal goes first to the course instructor, then the DUGC, then the Department Academic Appeals Board.

## Progression and degree
- All students are promoted to the next semester irrespective of performance. A student is expected to complete at least 85% of credits by the end of the 7th semester. The major project report can be submitted only after completing 122 credits (88 for lateral entry).
- Award of degree needs 160 credits (120 lateral entry), a final CGPA of at least 5.00, 100 activity points (75 for lateral entry) as per the AICTE activity point program, completion of mandatory non-credit courses (grade PP) and mandatory internships, and no dues or pending disciplinary action.
- The maximum time to complete the degree is 8 years (6 years for lateral entry). A student is terminated for failing to reach CGPA 5.0 or to earn the required credits in this period, for being absent for more than six weeks in a semester without permission, or for serious indiscipline.
- Honours: 18 additional credits in the same discipline. Eligibility: CGPA 7.5 or more and no backlog at the end of the 4th semester; Honours courses start from the 5th semester; final CGPA of 7.5 in the major and 7.0 in Honours courses is needed. Minor: 18 additional credits in another discipline; CGPA 5.0 or more and no backlog at the end of the 3rd semester; Minor courses start from the 4th semester. Up to 40% of Honours/Minor credits can come from MOOCs/SWAYAM/NPTEL.
- Temporary withdrawal for one semester is allowed once, for prolonged illness or a grave family calamity, if the student applies within 6 weeks of the start of the semester with documents and has no dues.

## Conduct and discipline
- Ragging in any form is a criminal offence and is banned; it is severely dealt with.
- Gross violations include ragging, indecent behaviour, damaging property, alcohol or drugs, hacking and cybercrime, plagiarism, use of mobile phones in the academic area, smoking and supari chewing, and unauthorized fundraising. Punishments range from reprimand and fines to debarring from exams, rustication or expulsion.
- Students must be inside the examination hall 20 minutes before the exam starts and are not allowed in after it begins. Unfair means in exams are reported to the Controller of Examinations.
